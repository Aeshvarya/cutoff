    How any of this actually works

    No maths required. Three ideas, and everything else follows from them.

    1

    Forgetting isn't random. It has a shape.

    Learn something today and you'll be shaky in a week, worse in a month. That fade follows a curve that's been measured since the 1880s. Because it has a shape, it can be predicted — that's the whole reason Cutoff can exist.

    2

    Every fact you know carries three numbers.

    This is the model in full. There is nothing else in it.

    How hard it is

    Some facts stick after one look. Others you re-learn five times. Tracked per fact, and it barely changes.

    How well it's stuck

    Days until you're down to a coin flip on it. A successful review pushes it up; forgetting knocks it back.

    Your chance right now

    Given how stuck it is and how long since you looked — could you produce it this second? This is what falls daily.

    3

    Revising late is worth more — until you run out of nights.

    For one fixed exam, later revision is worth more, simply because there's less time left for it to fade. But you can't revise six hundred facts the night before. The nights are the scarce thing — and once you count them, there's a last day you can start.

    Where the AI actually is
    Cutoff learned those three numbers, and exactly how they move after every right and wrong answer, from half a million real study records. Nobody typed the rules in. That learned model draws every line here — and no chatbot is involved in any of it. A language model reads your pasted syllabus, and nothing else.

    We tested it on half a million real answers

    We took the study records of 40 real people, showed Cutoff only the early part of each history, and asked it to predict what they'd get right later — answers it had never seen.
