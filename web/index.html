      The last day you can still start

      Here's the idea. Pick any day and imagine you start revising then and work flat out every single night until the exam — as hard as you physically can. How much would you know on the day? That's the blue line.

      Start early and you're fine. Start late and there simply aren't enough nights left, no matter how hard you work. The day the blue line drops below your target is your cutoff — and it lands well before the exam.

      The least work that still gets you there

      Cutoff picks revision sessions one at a time, always choosing the one that adds the most to your exam-day score per minute spent. It stops the moment you hit your target — so this is close to the smallest amount of work that works.

      You don't have one exam. You have two.

      Mid-sems in September, end-sems in November — and only so many free evenings in between. Every hour you give to one is an hour the other doesn't get.

      So there is no single "best" plan. There's a trade. Drag the slider and watch the two scores move in opposite directions. Every dot on the chart is a real revision plan; you're choosing which one you want to live in.

      How any of this actually works

      No maths required. Three ideas, and then everything else on this site follows from them.

      1

      Forgetting isn't random. It has a shape.

      Learn something today and you'll be shaky on it in a week, worse in a month. That fade follows a curve that's been measured since the 1880s. Because it has a shape, it can be predicted — that's the whole reason Cutoff can exist.

      2

      Every fact you know carries three numbers.

      This is the model, in full. There's nothing else in it.

      How hard it is

      Some facts stick after one look. Others you re-learn five times. Cutoff tracks this per fact and it barely changes.

      How well it's stuck

      How many days until you're down to a coin-flip on it. Revising successfully pushes this up. Forgetting knocks it back down.

      Your chance right now

      Given how stuck it is and how long since you looked — what are the odds you could produce it this second? This is what falls every day you do nothing.

      3

      Revising late is worth more — until you run out of nights.

      For a single exam, revision done close to the day is worth more, simply because there's less time left for it to fade. That's why cramming works, and we're not going to pretend otherwise.

      But you can't revise 600 facts the night before. You get through maybe forty in a sitting. So the nights themselves are the scarce thing — and once you count them, there's a last day you can start and still finish in time. That day is your cutoff, and almost nobody knows theirs.

      Where the AI actually is
      Cutoff learned those three numbers, and exactly how they move after every right and wrong answer, from half a million real study records. Nobody typed the rules in. That learned model is what draws every line on this site — and no chatbot is involved in any of it.

      We tested it on half a million real answers

      A tool that predicts your memory is worthless if it's wrong, so here's the check. We took the study records of 40 real people, showed Cutoff only the early part of each person's history, and asked it to predict what they'd get right later — answers it had never seen. Then we compared its predictions to what actually happened.
